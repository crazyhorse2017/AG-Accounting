## Before you hire: employee or contractor?

The first question is whether the person you’re paying is an **employee** or an **independent contractor**. The IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship as a whole. If you control what the worker does and how they do it, they’re probably an employee. Treating an employee as a contractor can make you liable for unpaid employment taxes, penalties and interest. If you’re unsure, Form SS-8 asks the IRS to decide.

## Step 1: Get set up with the IRS

- **Employer Identification Number (EIN):** apply for free on IRS.gov if you don’t already have one.
- **EFTPS enrollment:** federal employment taxes are deposited electronically. Enroll in the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System before your first deposit is due, or use a payroll provider that deposits for you.

## Step 2: Get set up with Ohio

- **Employer withholding account** with the Ohio Department of Taxation, for state income tax withheld from wages.
- **Unemployment insurance account** with the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services.
- **New hire reporting:** Ohio requires employers to report new and rehired employees to the state’s new hire reporting program shortly after hire.
- **Workers’ compensation:** Ohio employers obtain coverage through the Ohio Bureau of Workers’ Compensation (or are approved to self-insure). Private workers’ compensation insurance isn’t an option for most Ohio employers.
- **Municipal income tax:** Ohio employers generally withhold city income tax for the city where the employee works. Register with that city or its collection agency, such as RITA or CCA.

## Step 3: Paperwork for each new employee

- **Form W-4:** the employee’s federal withholding certificate. Keep it on file; you don’t send it to the IRS except on request.
- **Ohio withholding form (IT 4):** the employee’s Ohio withholding certificate.
- **Form I-9:** verifies identity and work eligibility. The employee completes Section 1 by the first day of work; you review documents and complete Section 2 within three business days. Keep it on file.
- **Payroll details:** pay rate, schedule, and any pre-tax benefits.

## Step 4: Each payroll

From each paycheck you withhold:

- Federal income tax, based on the W-4
- Social Security tax of 6.2% (up to the annual wage base, $184,500 for 2026) and Medicare tax of 1.45%
- Additional Medicare tax of 0.9% on an employee’s wages over $200,000 in the year
- Ohio income tax, and usually city income tax

As the employer you also pay:

- A matching 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare
- Federal unemployment tax (FUTA)
- Ohio unemployment tax

## Step 5: Deposits and returns

**Deposits.** Federal income tax withheld plus Social Security and Medicare (both shares) are deposited on a **monthly** or **semiweekly** schedule, depending on how much you reported in a lookback period. New employers are generally monthly depositors. Very small amounts may be paid with the quarterly return instead.

**Returns.**

- **Form 941**, quarterly: due by the last day of the month after each quarter
- **Form 940**, annually: federal unemployment tax, due January 31
- **Forms W-2 and W-3**: give employees their W-2 and file with the Social Security Administration by **January 31**
- Ohio and city withholding returns and reconciliations, on their schedules
- Ohio unemployment contribution reports, quarterly

## Step 6: Keep records

Keep employment tax records for at least **four years** after the tax is due or paid: wages, tips, withholding, deposits, W-4s, and copies of returns.

## Payroll services

Many small businesses use a payroll service or software. They can calculate withholding, make deposits and file returns. You remain responsible for the taxes, so choose a reputable provider, review the reports, and confirm deposits in EFTPS.

## Common first-year mistakes

1. Paying an employee as a contractor
2. Missing the first deposit deadline
3. Forgetting city income tax withholding
4. Not reporting the new hire to Ohio
5. Mixing payroll tax money with operating cash
