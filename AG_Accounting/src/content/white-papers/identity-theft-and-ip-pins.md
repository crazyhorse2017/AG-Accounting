## What tax identity theft is

Tax-related identity theft happens when someone uses your stolen Social Security number to file a fake return and claim a refund, or uses your number to get a job. Victims usually find out when their own return is rejected or when the IRS sends a letter.

## Warning signs

- Your e-filed return is **rejected** because a return with your Social Security number was already filed.
- You get an IRS letter about a return you **didn’t file**.
- An IRS notice shows **income from an employer** you don’t work for.
- You get a tax transcript or refund you didn’t request.
- The IRS sends a letter asking you to **verify your identity**.

## If it happens to you

1. **Respond to any IRS letter right away**, following its instructions. If the IRS asks you to verify your identity, do so using the method in the letter or on IRS.gov.
2. **If your e-filed return was rejected** because your number was already used, file your return on paper and attach **Form 14039, Identity Theft Affidavit**, unless the IRS has told you not to.
3. **Keep paying and filing** as usual. Identity theft doesn’t change what you owe.
4. **Protect your other accounts:** place a fraud alert or credit freeze with the credit bureaus, review your credit reports, and change passwords.
5. **Get an IP PIN** so it’s harder for anyone to file in your name again.

IRS identity theft cases can take time to resolve. Keep copies of everything you send and receive.

## The Identity Protection PIN

An **Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)** is a six-digit number known only to you and the IRS. Once you have one, a return filed with your Social Security number must include the correct IP PIN, or it will be rejected or delayed. That blocks thieves from filing in your name.

- **Who can get one:** anyone with a Social Security number or ITIN who can verify their identity.
- **How:** the fastest way is online through the IRS’s Get an IP PIN tool. There are other options for people who can’t verify online.
- **A new PIN every year:** the IRS issues a new IP PIN each filing season. Use the current one on your return.
- **Spouses and dependents:** each person on the return can have their own IP PIN, and each must be entered.
- **Never share it** with anyone except your trusted tax preparer, and never by email or text. The IRS won’t call, text or email to ask for it.

## How thieves get your information

- **Phishing** emails and texts that pretend to be from the IRS, a tax software company or a bank
- **Fake phone calls** demanding information or payment
- **Data breaches** at businesses
- **Lost or stolen mail and documents**

## Protecting yourself

- Don’t click links in unexpected tax-related emails or texts. Type **irs.gov** into your browser yourself.
- Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication on financial and tax accounts.
- Shred documents containing your Social Security number.
- File early, before a thief can.
- Get an IP PIN for everyone in your household.
