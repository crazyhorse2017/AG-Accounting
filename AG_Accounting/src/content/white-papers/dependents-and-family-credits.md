## Why dependents matter

Claiming a dependent can unlock several valuable tax benefits: the Child Tax Credit, the credit for other dependents, head of household filing status, the child and dependent care credit, and the Earned Income Tax Credit. Each benefit has its own rules, so a person may count for one and not another.

## Qualifying child

A **qualifying child** must meet all of these tests:

1. **Relationship:** your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, sibling, half-sibling, step-sibling, or a descendant of any of them (such as a grandchild, niece or nephew).
2. **Age:** under 19 at the end of the year, or under 24 if a full-time student for at least five months, or any age if permanently and totally disabled. The child must also be younger than you (or your spouse, if filing jointly), unless disabled.
3. **Residency:** lived with you for more than half the year. Temporary absences, such as school, count as time at home. Special rules apply to children of divorced or separated parents.
4. **Support:** the child didn’t provide more than half of their own support.
5. **Joint return:** the child isn’t filing a joint return, except only to claim a refund.

## Qualifying relative

A **qualifying relative** is someone who isn’t anyone’s qualifying child and who:

- Is related to you in one of the ways listed in IRS rules (such as a parent, grandparent, or in-law), **or** lived with you all year as a member of your household,
- Had gross income below a yearly limit, and
- Received more than half of their total support from you.

Elderly parents are a common example.

## Tie-breaker rules

If a child could be the qualifying child of more than one person, only one can claim the child. Generally:

- A **parent** wins over a non-parent.
- If both parents claim the child and don’t file jointly, the parent the child lived with **longer** wins; if equal, the parent with the **higher adjusted gross income** wins.
- If no parent claims the child, the person with the highest AGI wins, but only if it’s higher than any parent’s.

## The credits

**Child Tax Credit.** Up to **$2,200** per qualifying child under age **17** for 2026, with up to **$1,700** refundable. The child must have a valid Social Security number, and generally the taxpayer must too. The credit phases out above **$200,000** of modified AGI (**$400,000** for joint filers).

**Credit for other dependents.** Up to **$500** for dependents who don’t qualify for the Child Tax Credit, such as children 17 and older, college students, or elderly parents. It isn’t refundable.

**Child and dependent care credit.** Helps pay for care of a child under 13, or a spouse or dependent who can’t care for themselves, so you can work or look for work. Qualifying expenses are capped at **$3,000** for one person and **$6,000** for two or more. Starting in 2026, the credit percentage for lower-income families can reach 50% of qualifying expenses. You must report the provider’s name, address and taxpayer ID. Day camp counts; overnight camp doesn’t.

**Earned Income Tax Credit.** A refundable credit for workers with low to moderate income. The EITC has its own definition of a qualifying child (with no support test). For 2026, the maximum credit is **$664** with no children, **$4,427** with one, **$7,316** with two, and **$8,231** with three or more.

## Head of household

An unmarried person who pays more than half the cost of keeping up a home for a qualifying person can file as **head of household**, which brings a larger standard deduction (**$24,150** for 2026) and wider tax brackets than single status.

## Common mistakes

- Two people claiming the same child
- Claiming a child who didn’t live with you more than half the year
- Missing the credit for other dependents for older children and relatives
- Forgetting the care provider’s taxpayer ID
- Assuming a child who qualifies for one credit qualifies for all of them

## Records to keep

- Birth certificates or other proof of relationship
- School, medical or lease records showing where a child lived
- Records of support you provided
- Child care receipts with the provider’s information

AG Accounting can help you confirm which dependents and credits you can claim.
