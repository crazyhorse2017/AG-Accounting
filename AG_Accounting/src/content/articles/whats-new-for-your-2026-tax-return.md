Every year the IRS adjusts tax brackets and many other amounts for inflation, and the tax law passed in July 2025 added several new deductions. Here is what is different for the 2026 tax year, the return most people will file in early 2027.

## A bigger standard deduction

The standard deduction for 2026 is **$16,100** for single filers and married people filing separately, **$24,150** for heads of household, and **$32,200** for married couples filing jointly.

If you are 65 or older, or blind, you add **$2,050** if you are unmarried or **$1,650** for each qualifying spouse if you are married.

## Brackets moved up

The seven tax rates stay the same, from 10% to 37%, but the income ranges for each rate went up. For example, a single filer pays 10% on the first $12,400 of taxable income, and the 22% rate starts above $50,400. You can see every bracket on our [2026 tax rates page](/tax-center/rates).

## New deductions for 2025 through 2028

These deductions are available whether or not you itemize, but each has income limits and rules.

- **Seniors:** people 65 and older may deduct an extra **$6,000** each. It phases out for modified adjusted gross income over $75,000 ($150,000 for joint filers).
- **Tips:** workers in jobs that customarily receive tips may deduct up to **$25,000** of qualified tips.
- **Overtime:** workers may deduct up to **$12,500** of qualified overtime pay ($25,000 for joint filers). It phases out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint).
- **Car loan interest:** interest on a loan for a qualifying new personal vehicle may be deductible, up to **$10,000** a year.

## Charitable gifts without itemizing

Starting with 2026, people who take the standard deduction may deduct up to **$1,000** of cash gifts to qualified charities, or **$2,000** for married couples filing jointly. Keep your receipts.

## Credits for families

The Child Tax Credit is worth up to **$2,200** per qualifying child, with up to $1,700 refundable. The maximum Earned Income Tax Credit for 2026 is **$8,231** for families with three or more qualifying children.

## What to do now

1. Check your withholding with the [IRS Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator), especially if you earn tips or overtime.
2. Keep pay stubs or records that show tips and overtime separately.
3. Save charity receipts, even if you don’t itemize.
4. Use our [appointment checklist](/tax-center/appointment-checklist) to gather your documents early.
