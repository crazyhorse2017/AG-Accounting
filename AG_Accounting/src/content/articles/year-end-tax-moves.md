Most tax planning has to happen before December 31. Once the year ends, your options mostly shrink to what you can claim on the return. Here are moves worth a look each fall.

## 1. Check your withholding

If you changed jobs, got a raise, picked up side income, or had a big life change, your withholding may be off. The [IRS Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator) shows whether you’re on track. A W-4 change in November or December still applies to your remaining paychecks.

## 2. Add to workplace retirement plans

401(k), 403(b), and most 457 contributions have to come out of a paycheck by December 31. For 2026, you can contribute up to **$24,500**, plus **$8,000** more if you’re 50 or older, or **$11,250** if you’re 60 through 63. Traditional contributions lower your taxable income now.

IRA contributions are different: you have until the filing deadline, usually April 15, to contribute for 2026.

## 3. Take required minimum distributions

If you’re required to take a minimum distribution this year, the deadline is generally **December 31**. (Your very first one can wait until April 1 of the next year, but then you’ll take two in one year.) Missing an RMD can bring a steep additional tax. Our [RMD calculator](/calculators/required-minimum-distribution) estimates the amount.

## 4. Review investment gains and losses

If you sold investments at a gain, selling others at a loss can offset that gain. If losses are more than gains, up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 if married filing separately) can offset other income, and the rest carries forward. Be careful with the wash sale rule if you buy the same investment back within 30 days.

## 5. Plan your charitable giving

Starting with 2026, people who don’t itemize may deduct up to **$1,000** of cash gifts to qualified charities (**$2,000** for joint filers). If you do itemize, grouping two years of gifts into one year can sometimes push you over the standard deduction. Always get a written acknowledgment for gifts of $250 or more.

## 6. Use up flexible spending money

Many health FSA plans have a use-it-or-lose-it rule or a short grace period. Check your balance and your plan’s deadline.

## 7. Gather records now, not in April

Start a folder for this year’s tax documents and add to it as they arrive in January. Our [appointment checklist](/tax-center/appointment-checklist) lists what to look for.

> Not every move fits every situation. A quick conversation before year-end can show which of these are worth doing for you.
