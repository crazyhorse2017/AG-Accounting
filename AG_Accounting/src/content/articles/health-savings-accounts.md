A Health Savings Account (HSA) is one of the most tax-friendly accounts available. If you qualify, it can be a powerful way to pay for medical costs and save for later.

## The three tax benefits

1. **Contributions lower your taxes.** They’re either deductible or made before tax through payroll.
2. **Growth is tax-free.** Interest and investment earnings aren’t taxed while in the account.
3. **Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.**

## Who can contribute

You generally can contribute to an HSA if you:

- Are covered by a **high-deductible health plan** (HDHP),
- Have no other health coverage that disqualifies you,
- Aren’t enrolled in Medicare, and
- Can’t be claimed as a dependent on someone else’s return.

The yearly contribution limits depend on whether you have self-only or family coverage, with an extra catch-up amount if you’re 55 or older.

## Using the money

HSA money can pay for qualified medical expenses for you, your spouse and your dependents, such as doctor visits, prescriptions, dental care and eyeglasses. [Publication 502](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-502) lists what counts.

If you use HSA money for something that isn’t a qualified medical expense, it’s taxable, and before age 65 there’s generally an extra **20%** tax. After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed like traditional IRA withdrawals, without the extra tax.

## It’s yours to keep

Unlike many flexible spending accounts, an HSA **isn’t use-it-or-lose-it**. The balance rolls over year after year and stays with you if you change jobs.

## Smart ways to use an HSA

- **Contribute through payroll** if your employer offers it. Those contributions also avoid Social Security and Medicare tax.
- **Save receipts.** You can reimburse yourself later for qualified expenses you paid out of pocket, as long as they were after the HSA was set up.
- **Invest for the long term** if you can pay current medical costs from other money. An HSA can act as an extra retirement account for future health costs.

## Reporting

You report HSA contributions and withdrawals on **Form 8889** with your return. Your HSA provider sends Forms 5498-SA and 1099-SA.
