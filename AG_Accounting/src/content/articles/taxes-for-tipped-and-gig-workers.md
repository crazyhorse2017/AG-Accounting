Tips and gig income are both taxable, but they’re handled in different ways. Knowing the rules can save you from a surprise bill.

## If you earn tips as an employee

All tips are income, whether they’re cash, added to a card, or shared with coworkers. If you receive **$20 or more in tips in a month** at a job, you must report them to your employer, who then withholds tax on them.

For 2025 through 2028, there is a new deduction for **qualified tips of up to $25,000** a year, for people in jobs where tips are customary. The deduction has income limits and other rules, and it reduces income tax only. Social Security and Medicare taxes still apply to tips. Keep a daily tip log so you can show how much you received.

## If you drive, deliver, or freelance

Most gig workers are **independent contractors**, not employees. That means:

- No one withholds tax from your pay.
- You pay **self-employment tax** of 15.3% on most of your net earnings, on top of income tax.
- You may need to make **quarterly estimated tax payments**.

Platforms may send you a Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K, but your income is taxable even if you don’t get a form.

## Deduct your real costs

As a contractor, you pay tax on profit, not on everything you’re paid. Common deductions include:

- Business miles driven, using the standard mileage rate or actual car expenses
- Phone and data costs for the share used for work
- Platform fees and commissions
- Supplies, equipment, and insulated bags or other gear

Keep a mileage log and save receipts. Our [mileage deduction calculator](/calculators/mileage-deduction) and [self-employment tax calculator](/calculators/self-employment-tax) can help you plan.

## Don’t forget Ohio and your city

Ohio taxes your income, and most Ohio cities have their own income tax. Gig workers usually owe city tax where they live, and sometimes where they work, and no one withholds it for them. Our [city income tax calculator](/calculators/ohio-municipal-income-tax) gives a rough estimate.

## Set money aside as you go

A simple habit: move a set percentage of every payout into a separate savings account for taxes. Many people start with 25% to 30% and adjust once they see their first full year.
