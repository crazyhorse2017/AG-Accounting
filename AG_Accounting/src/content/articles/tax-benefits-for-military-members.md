Members of the armed forces have the same basic tax rules as everyone else, plus several special benefits. [Publication 3](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-3), the IRS’s Armed Forces’ Tax Guide, covers them in detail.

## Pay that isn’t taxed

- **Combat pay** earned while serving in a combat zone is generally excluded from income. For officers, the exclusion is limited.
- Many **allowances** aren’t taxable, including housing (BAH) and subsistence (BAS) allowances, and certain family and moving allowances.

## Extra time to file and pay

Service members in a **combat zone** or a qualified hazardous duty area generally get extra time to file returns and pay taxes, usually at least **180 days after leaving** the area, plus any time that was left to file when they entered.

## Moving expenses

Most people can no longer deduct moving expenses, but **active-duty members who move because of a permanent change of station** still can.

## The Earned Income Tax Credit

You can choose to include nontaxable combat pay in earned income when figuring the EITC. Doing so can increase the credit for some families. You can run it both ways and pick the better result.

## Reservists

Members of the reserves who travel **more than 100 miles** from home for drills or meetings may be able to deduct their travel expenses, even if they don’t itemize.

## Selling a home

If you’re on qualified extended duty, you can choose to suspend the five-year test period for the home sale exclusion for up to 10 years, making it easier to qualify.

## Free tax help

Military members and their families can get free tax preparation and filing help through military installation tax centers and the Defense Department’s MilTax program.

## Ohio

Ohio offers its own tax benefits for some military pay. Rules depend on where you’re stationed and your residency, so check the Ohio Department of Taxation or ask us.
