The qualified business income (QBI) deduction, sometimes called the Section 199A deduction, lets many small business owners deduct up to **20%** of their qualified business income. It was created in 2017 and the 2025 tax law made it permanent.

## Who can claim it

The deduction is for owners of **pass-through** businesses:

- Sole proprietors and single-member LLCs
- Partners in partnerships and multi-member LLCs
- S corporation shareholders
- Some trusts and estates

C corporations don’t get it. Wages you earn as an employee don’t count as qualified business income.

## How it’s taken

The QBI deduction is taken **after** adjusted gross income is figured, and you can claim it whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. It lowers your **income tax**, but not your self-employment tax.

## The basic calculation

For many owners, the deduction is simply 20% of qualified business income, limited to 20% of taxable income (minus net capital gains). Starting in 2026, there’s also a **minimum deduction of $400** for taxpayers with at least **$1,000** of qualified business income.

## Limits at higher incomes

Once taxable income goes above certain thresholds, two kinds of limits begin to apply:

- **Specified service businesses**, such as health, law, accounting, consulting, financial services, and others where the main asset is the owners’ skill, see the deduction phase out.
- For other businesses, the deduction can be limited based on **W-2 wages paid** and the **cost of business property**.

Below those income thresholds, these limits don’t apply.

## What counts as qualified business income

QBI is generally the net income from a qualified U.S. business. It doesn’t include:

- Capital gains and losses
- Most interest and dividend income
- Reasonable compensation paid to you by your S corporation
- Guaranteed payments from a partnership

## Planning points

- Choosing between a sole proprietorship and an S corporation can change your QBI deduction, because S corporation wages you pay yourself aren’t QBI.
- Retirement contributions and other deductions can affect both QBI and the taxable income limit.
- Rental real estate can sometimes qualify if it rises to the level of a business.
