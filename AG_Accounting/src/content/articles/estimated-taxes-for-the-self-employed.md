When you work for an employer, tax comes out of every paycheck. When you work for yourself, nobody does that for you. The tax system is pay-as-you-go, so the IRS expects you to send in tax during the year through **estimated tax payments**.

## Who needs to pay

Generally, you should make estimated payments if you expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when you file, after subtracting withholding and refundable credits. That often includes:

- Sole proprietors, independent contractors, and gig workers
- Partners and S corporation shareholders
- People with large investment income, rental income, or alimony from older agreements
- Retirees whose pensions or retirement withdrawals don’t have enough withheld

## What the payments cover

Your estimated payments cover both **income tax** and **self-employment tax**. Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of your net self-employment earnings. It applies once those earnings reach $400. Half of it is deductible when you figure your adjusted gross income.

Try our [self-employment tax calculator](/calculators/self-employment-tax) to see your number.

## When payments are due

The four payments are usually due on **April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15** of the following year. When a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. Our [tax due dates page](/tax-center/due-dates) lists the exact dates.

Ohio and many Ohio cities also expect estimated payments if you’ll owe them tax, generally on similar dates.

## How much to pay: the safe harbor

You can generally avoid an underpayment penalty if, during the year, you pay in at least the smaller of:

1. **90%** of the tax you’ll owe for this year, or
2. **100%** of the tax shown on last year’s return (**110%** if last year’s adjusted gross income was more than $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).

Using last year’s tax is often the easiest approach because you already know the number. Divide it by four and pay that each quarter. Our [quarterly estimated tax calculator](/calculators/quarterly-estimated-tax) does the math.

## How to pay

You can pay federal estimates through IRS Direct Pay, your IRS Online Account, the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS), or by mail with Form 1040-ES vouchers. See [IRS payment options](https://www.irs.gov/payments).

## Tips that make it easier

- Set aside a fixed percentage of every payment you receive in a separate savings account.
- If you also have a job, you can increase withholding on your W-4 instead of sending estimates. Withholding counts as if it were paid evenly through the year.
- If your income is uneven, the annualized income method may lower or delay required payments.

> Missed a payment? Pay as soon as you can. The penalty is figured like interest, so it stops growing once you’ve paid.
