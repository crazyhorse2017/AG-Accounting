If you owe more tax than you can pay right now, you’re not alone, and you have choices. The most important thing is not to ignore it.

## Step 1: File on time anyway

The penalty for **not filing** is generally much bigger than the penalty for **not paying**: 5% of the unpaid tax per month versus 0.5% per month (each up to 25%). Filing on time, or getting an extension, avoids the larger penalty even if you can’t pay.

## Step 2: Pay what you can

Interest and the failure-to-pay penalty are figured on the unpaid balance, so paying part now lowers what you’ll owe later.

## Step 3: Choose a payment option

- **Short-term payment plan:** if you can pay in full within **180 days**, you may qualify for more time.
- **Long-term payment plan (installment agreement):** pay monthly over time. Many people can set one up online. While a plan is in effect, the failure-to-pay penalty rate is reduced.
- **Offer in compromise:** in some cases, the IRS will settle for less than the full amount if paying in full would cause financial hardship. It has strict requirements, so check eligibility first.
- **Temporary delay:** if you truly can’t pay anything right now, the IRS may delay collection until your situation improves, though interest continues.

## Step 4: Ask about penalty relief

If you have a clean history, you may qualify for **first-time penalty relief**. Penalties may also be removed for reasonable cause, such as serious illness or a disaster.

## Watch out for “tax relief” scams

Be careful with companies that advertise they can settle your tax debt for “pennies on the dollar.” Many charge large fees for things you could do yourself or that you don’t qualify for.

## Don’t forget Ohio and your city

Ohio and your city tax agency have their own payment options. Contact them separately if you also owe state or city tax.

## Plan for next year

Once the balance is handled, adjust your W-4 withholding or make estimated payments so it doesn’t happen again.
