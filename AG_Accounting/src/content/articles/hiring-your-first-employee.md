Hiring your first employee is a big step. It also makes you responsible for withholding and paying several taxes. Here’s a checklist to get set up correctly.

## Before the first paycheck

1. **Get an Employer Identification Number (EIN)** from the IRS if you don’t have one. It’s free.
2. **Have the employee complete Form W-4** so you know how much federal income tax to withhold.
3. **Complete Form I-9** to verify the employee is eligible to work in the U.S. You keep it on file; it isn’t sent to the IRS.
4. **Report the new hire** to Ohio’s new hire reporting program.
5. **Register with Ohio** for employer income tax withholding and for state unemployment insurance.
6. **Set up workers’ compensation coverage** through the Ohio Bureau of Workers’ Compensation.
7. **Check city income tax rules**, because Ohio employers generally withhold municipal income tax.

## What you withhold and pay

- **Federal income tax**, based on the employee’s W-4
- **Social Security and Medicare** (7.65% from the employee, matched by you)
- **Ohio income tax** and, usually, **city income tax**
- **Federal unemployment tax (FUTA)**, paid by you only, not withheld
- **Ohio unemployment tax**, paid by you

## Paying and filing

- **Deposit** withheld and employer taxes on time, usually through EFTPS, the federal tax payment system. Your deposit schedule (monthly or semiweekly) depends on the size of your payroll.
- File **Form 941** each quarter to report federal withholding and Social Security and Medicare taxes.
- File **Form 940** each year for federal unemployment tax.
- Give employees their **Form W-2** and file it with the government by **January 31**.

Ohio and your city have their own filing schedules for withholding returns.

## Keep payroll records

Keep records of wages, tax withheld, deposits, and payroll returns for at least **four years**.

## Employee or contractor?

Before you hire, make sure the worker really is an employee. Treating an employee as an independent contractor can lead to back taxes and penalties. See our article on [employee vs. contractor](/articles/employee-or-contractor).

## Consider a payroll service

Many small businesses use a payroll service or software to calculate withholding, make deposits and file returns. You’re still responsible if it isn’t done right, so review the reports.
