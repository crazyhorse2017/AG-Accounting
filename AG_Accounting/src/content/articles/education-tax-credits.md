If you or a family member pays for college or job-related courses, a tax credit may cover part of the cost. There are two education credits, and you claim either one on **Form 8863**.

## American Opportunity Tax Credit (AOTC)

- Worth up to **$2,500 per eligible student** per year: 100% of the first $2,000 of qualified expenses plus 25% of the next $2,000.
- Up to **40%** of it (up to $1,000) is **refundable**, so you can get it back even if you owe no tax.
- Only for the **first four years** of college, and the student must be pursuing a degree or credential and enrolled at least half-time for at least one academic period.
- Can be claimed for each eligible student, so a family with two students in college may claim it twice.

## Lifetime Learning Credit (LLC)

- Worth up to **$2,000 per tax return**: 20% of up to $10,000 of qualified expenses.
- **Not refundable.**
- No limit on the number of years, and it covers undergraduate, graduate, and professional courses, including classes taken to improve job skills.

## Income limits

Both credits phase out as income rises. They begin to shrink when modified adjusted gross income is over **$80,000** ($160,000 for joint filers) and disappear at **$90,000** ($180,000 joint). Married people filing separately can’t claim either credit.

## What expenses count

Qualified expenses generally include tuition and required enrollment fees. For the AOTC, required course materials such as books also count. Room and board, insurance, and transportation don’t.

You can’t use the same expenses for both a credit and a tax-free benefit, such as a tax-free scholarship or a 529 plan withdrawal.

## Picking the right one

You can’t claim both credits for the same student in the same year. For an undergraduate in the first four years, the AOTC is usually worth more. For graduate school, part-time classes, or job training, the Lifetime Learning Credit is often the one that fits.

## Paperwork

The school sends **Form 1098-T**, but the credit is based on what you actually paid during the year. Keep receipts for tuition, fees and books.
